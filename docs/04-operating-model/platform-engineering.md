# Platform engineering

Platform Engineering remains important. Internal paved paths, developer
experience, and shared systems are how many capabilities are made usable.

## What does not disappear

- Building and operating internal platforms
- Golden paths, CI/CD substrates, environment baselines
- Product thinking about internal users (including agents as users)
- The hard work of integration across specialist systems

## What changes

The platform should be understood as an **enabling system for
capabilities**: discovery, composition, governance, and consumption. It is
not the final destination of software delivery and not the organizational
center of gravity.

A portal that routes the same tickets to the same queues is not Converged
Engineering. It is a skin on the supply chain.

Platform teams often become the place where composition happens
(`DeployApplication` as a product). That is appropriate when platform
engineers work *with* SRE, security, and infrastructure as providers of
lower capabilities, not as a layer that hides them behind a new silo.

## What this is not

Converged Engineering is not a rebrand of Platform Engineering. Platforms
are one of the ways capabilities are offered. An organization could encode
capabilities primarily as APIs and agent tools with a thin UI and still be
practicing the model. Conversely, a large platform org can still run on
handoffs. Staffing a platform team is not the same as collapsing delivery
boundaries.

The developer is not the platform's customer as the purpose of the firm.
The platform exists so product value can move. Measuring only NPS of the
portal repeats layer-oriented optimization.
