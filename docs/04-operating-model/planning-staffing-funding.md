# Planning, staffing, and funding

Converged Engineering changes what an organization plans around, who is
accountable for what, and where money draws boundaries. It does **not**
prescribe an org chart, a funding model, or a Convergence team.

This page states what changes. It deliberately stops short of prescribing
mechanism, because the mechanism depends on scale, risk, and context, and
because prescribing one would recreate the problem the model exists to
remove. Incentives and stewardship are stated in the
[operating model overview](README.md); this page builds on them.

## Planning

The unit of planning shifts from team output toward the path from intent
to outcome.

| Plans often optimize | Convergence asks them to also see |
| --- | --- |
| Each team's roadmap in isolation | The flow a class of intent takes across teams |
| Feature output as the measure | Whether intent reaches outcome, with expertise preserved |
| Encoding as a one-time project | Capabilities as owned things that decay and evolve |

Capability ownership has to appear in planning, not as an afterthought. A
capability with no one funded to maintain it becomes
[legacy on the day it ships](../12-method/deciding-what-to-encode.md).
Planning should also respect the split the method rests on: encode the
repeatable, staff the novel as collaboration. Planning every capability to
"done" misreads the novel work as a backlog.

## Staffing

Specialists remain, and specialization remains a career. Converged
Engineering adds a responsibility, not a job family.

| Not the change | The change |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone becomes full-stack | Capability ownership becomes an explicit responsibility |
| A new "Convergence" job title | Existing specialists own capabilities in their domain |
| A central team that approves capabilities | Domain authority stays with the domains |
| Collapsing specialties into product | Someone owns each composite, including its seams |

The composite is the staffing question people miss. `DeployApplication`
draws on several domains and belongs to none of them by default. Someone
has to own the seam, and that ownership is real work with no obvious home.
Naming that owner is a staffing decision, not a new department.

**Convergence cannot be delegated to a Convergence function.** A
transformation effort may have stewardship, but Converged Engineering is a
property of the engineering system, not another delivery specialty. See
[Convergence as a function](../08-anti-patterns/convergence-as-a-function.md).

## Funding

Money draws boundaries as surely as the org chart does, and the model has
to be honest about that.

| Funding failure mode | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Budgets set strictly per team | Cross-cutting capabilities and seams go unfunded |
| Platform adoption as a funded target | The platform optimizes for adoption, not for outcome |
| Local budget optimization | A locally cheap choice that is expensive for the system |
| Vendor and commercial contracts | Delivery boundaries encoded outside the org entirely |

**Convergence requires incentive compatibility, not merely technical
compatibility. A capability must not exist to justify its realization.
Commercial structures can become delivery boundaries just as
organizational structures can.**

## What this page does not prescribe

- No budgeting, chargeback, showback, or cost-allocation model.
- No compensation or performance framework.
- No procurement model.
- No org structure, headcount formula, or team topology.

These are named as [open questions](../00-foundations/open-questions.md),
not because they do not matter but because there is not yet enough evidence
to prescribe one honestly. Stewardship should exist at the level where the
concern exists, and no further.
