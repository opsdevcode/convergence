# Distribute execution. Preserve accountability.

**Major Principle 5 of Convergence** (working doctrine, v0.1)

Who performs an action can be distributed across people, automation,
platforms, and compositions. Who is accountable must remain explicit.

**Shared contribution does not require shared accountability.**

**Accountability does not require centralized implementation control.**

**Accountability includes responsibility for learning and evolution, not
merely conformance.**

## Distinct ideas (not an IAM model)

| Idea | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Authority** | Who determines what correct means within a domain or decision scope? |
| **Ownership** | Who is accountable for stewardship and continued fitness of a capability? |
| **Execution** | Who or what performs an action? |
| **Privilege** | What is an actor technically permitted to do? |
| **Outcome ownership** | Who owns the result closest to the relevant intent? |

**Authority does not imply execution.**

**Execution does not imply authority.**

**Ownership is not itself a security boundary.**

Distributed execution must not silently expand authority through
[capability composition](../02-capabilities/composite-capabilities.md).
Invoking a capability, including by an agent, does not grant domain
authority. Privilege is a separate control problem. This principle does
not define an identity or access-control framework.

See [Ownership and authority](../02-capabilities/ownership-and-authority.md).

## What this principle does not mean

- Centralize all implementation.
- Collapse specialists into one accountable "full stack" team.
- Hide owners because consumers do not route through them.
- Treat capability ownership as a substitute for authorization.
- Give AI agents authority because they can call a tool.

See [Major Principle 3](03-organization-is-an-implementation-detail.md).
