# Capability as a team rename

The database team becomes `ProvideRelationalStorage`. The intake process,
the queue, the lead times, and the tribal knowledge are unchanged. A
capability register is produced listing existing teams in verb form.

## Why it is tempting

It requires no change and produces immediate visible progress. A list of
capabilities can be assembled in an afternoon by renaming the org chart,
and it looks like adoption.

It is also a natural mistake. Teams genuinely do hold the expertise
behind capabilities, so mapping one to the other feels correct.

## What it costs

| Cost | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Vocabulary inflation | The word "capability" stops meaning anything locally |
| Blocked diagnosis | Naming the team as the capability hides that the team is the interface |
| Reorganization fragility | Capability names now die at the next reorganization |
| Lost distinction | Experience, realization, and capability collapse into one thing |

## How to recognize it

- Capability names map one to one onto current team names.
- Each capability's realization is "the team".
- Consumers still need to know which capability belongs to which team,
  because that determines how to ask.
- Renaming was the entire change.

A useful test: if the team were dissolved tomorrow and its people
distributed, would the capability name still describe something the
organization can do? If not, it is a team with a verb.

## What to do instead

Name what the system can accomplish, not who does it. See
[identifying capabilities](../12-method/identifying-capabilities.md) for
naming guidance, and note that a capability's
[realization](../02-capabilities/realization.md) can be a team without
the capability being that team.

Then do the work the rename skipped: find which parts of the interaction
are settled expertise and which are judgment.
